What actually moves New York rents

Three connected models for thinking about the same question from different angles: how much housing New York needs, and which levers — building, transit, and how fast we run it — actually change rents, condition, and who gets to stay. Each is a reasoning aid, not a forecast: every assumption is a dial you can turn, sourced to the empirical record.

NYC Rental Supply Simulator

Through-Running Housing Model

Run Penn Station's three railroads through instead of terminating, and every regional home reaches far more jobs in the same time — unlocking housing markets across the region and bleeding demand off NYC. Operations, not construction: the cheapest, fastest lever. After ETA & Momentum.

What these share

Dials, not verdicts

Every contested number is adjustable. If you distrust a coefficient, change it and watch — the tools are built to be interrogated, not believed.

Sourced to the record

Defaults are anchored to published research — HVS, the Transit Costs Project, ETA, and more — with the citations listed inside each model.

Honest about limits

They show costs alongside benefits, name what they can't see, and stay directional. A reasoning aid that flatters one answer isn't one.
