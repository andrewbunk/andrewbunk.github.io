Through-running stitches the job market

Penn Station's three railroads terminate instead of connecting, so a home in New Jersey can't easily reach Long Island or the far side of Manhattan. Run the trains through — same tracks, same stations — and every regional home reaches far more jobs in the same time. Housing gets built where jobs are reachable, which unlocks new markets across the region and bleeds demand off the NYC rental market. This is operations, not construction, so it's fast and cheap.

Sources & how the model works

Built on the two proposals you named, with job accessibility — "how many jobs can you reach in 45 minutes" — as the spine, the standard measure in regional transit-and-land-use planning.

  1. Effective Transit Alliance — Through-Running: First Connections: the phased plan. Phase 1 at Penn Station Access opening (2028) with no new infrastructure; Phase 2 early 2030s adds LIRR branches with the tri-voltage M10; Phase 3 with Gateway (~2035–38) reaches 48 tph; Phase 4 links Penn to Grand Central and "would more than double the number of jobs within the stations' walksheds."
  2. Nolan Hicks — Momentum (NYU Transit Costs Project): electrification, high platforms, and common lightweight EMUs — the rolling-stock modernization that lets frequency scale across the region.
  3. RPA — Penn Station constraints and ETA on the FRA Phase I report (2026): the binding constraints — rolling-stock compatibility, yard/turning capacity, and governance across MTA, NJT, and Amtrak.

The dial raises job access two ways that compound: one-seat reach (through-running removes transfers, connecting to the far side of the region) and frequency (a train every 10–15 min instead of hourly makes the same track a place to live car-free), both gated by governance. Housing unlocked scales with the job-access gain and with upzoning. Illustrative and directional — calibrated to the proposals' own phasing and the "more than double the walkshed" figure, not a ridership model.

How this connects. Unlike the transit-delivery model, through-running builds almost nothing — it operates existing track as one network, so it's the cheapest and fastest of the three housing levers. Its output is overwhelmingly suburban and outer-borough capacity, plus relief on the NYC rental market as demand that would have crowded into the city spreads to now-reachable regional locations.
